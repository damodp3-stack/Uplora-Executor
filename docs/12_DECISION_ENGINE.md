# Uplora: Decision Engine & Strategic Governance

## 1. The Strategic Pivot Hazard
Founders of early-stage service and commerce companies frequently suffer from "pivot whiplash" — abandoning profitable, foundational revenue streams (like website design) the moment a shiny new concept (like autonomous dropshipping or SaaS) appears.

The **Decision Engine** acts as an objective strategic circuit breaker.

---

## 2. Strategic Change Analysis Protocol
Whenever Damo or a team member enters a major company redirection (e.g., *"Stop custom website services and migrate 100% of engineering to StoreIK"*), the system enforces a 6-dimension risk impact audit:

1. **Immediate Cashflow Risk**:
   * *Analysis*: Website projects currently deliver ~₹40,000/month (100% of operational revenue). Severing this eliminates immediate runway.
2. **Customer / Contractual Exposure**:
   * *Analysis*: Existing clients requiring maintenance or upcoming retainer upsells will be stranded.
3. **Team Capacity Re-allocation**:
   * *Analysis*: Does Partner have clear product specifications for StoreIK? Does Assistant know how to prospect SaaS merchants instead of local businesses?
4. **Capital & Burn Requirements**:
   * *Analysis*: How many months can Uplora survive at ₹0 revenue while StoreIK builds momentum?
5. **Constructive Alternative**:
   * *Recommendation*: Retain 65% capacity for website cash generation; allocate 35% time (Fridays & Saturdays) to StoreIK MVP sprints.
6. **Time-Boxed Pilot Requirement**:
   * Propose a 14-day validation pilot with 3 pilot merchants before any service discontinuation.

---

## 3. Human-in-the-Loop Governance
The AI cannot unilaterally execute strategic changes. The founder must formally execute one of three explicit commands:
* **[APPROVE FULL CHANGE]**: Overrides cautions and logs permanent pivot in Company Memory.
* **[REJECT PROPOSAL]**: Retains current status quo.
* **[INITIATE 14-DAY VALIDATION PILOT]**: Creates an isolated sandbox experiment with success criteria.
